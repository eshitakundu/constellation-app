From letters to light

How your constellation takes shape

01

A star for each character

Each letter, space, or symbol becomes a star. Its character code and position in your phrase determine where it appears. Changing the order of your words changes the map.

02

The shortest next connection

Starting with the first star, the generator repeatedly connects the nearest unconnected star to the growing pattern. This minimum spanning tree joins the stars without forming loops.

03

A pattern you can return to

The calculation is repeatable: the same text produces the same star positions and connections. Capital letters become lowercase and spaces at the beginning and end are removed.

The background sky is decorative. Your generated stars use fixed coordinates, so the pattern scales to fit your screen. Some characters can land close together or overlap; the formula creates playful patterns, not a unique identifier.

A few things to know

Questions about your constellation

Is this a real constellation or birth chart?

No. This is generative art based on text. It does not use astronomical observations, your birthday, or your location, and it makes no predictions about personality or the future.

Why does the same name always make the same pattern?

The generator uses a fixed mathematical rule instead of random star positions. Case does not matter, but punctuation, internal spaces, and the order of characters do. The animated background is separate from your pattern.

What happens with a single letter or emoji?

A single Unicode code point creates one star with no connecting lines. Some emoji consist of several code points and can create several stars. The input accepts up to 60 characters as counted by your browser.

Can I download and share the result?

Download PNG saves a 1600 × 1000 image with your complete constellation and its title. Copy share link creates a URL that recreates your text and pattern. The link contains your text, so avoid sharing private information.

Do you save the names I enter?

The generator processes text in your browser without sending it to a generation server. There is no account or saved gallery. Shared text is stored in the link’s fragment. Read our privacy notice for hosting and external-link details.
